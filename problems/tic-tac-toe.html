Tic-Tac-Toe

Two games of tic-tac-toe are counted as distinct if the same final board is reached via different move orderings. Let $M_n$ denote the number of games that end on move $n$ (someone wins, or the board fills up on move $9$). How many distinct tic-tac-toe games are there in total, expressed in terms of the $M_n$?

Solution

If we let both players keep marking even after a win, there are $9!$ orderings of who plays which square. But many of those orderings correspond to "games" that should have already ended.

For each game that legitimately ends on move $n < 9$, the remaining $9 - n$ squares can be filled in $(9 - n)!$ orderings — and exactly one of those orderings is the legitimate game; the other $(9 - n)! - 1$ are illegal continuations counted by the $9!$. The smallest $n$ at which anyone can win is $n = 5$.

Subtracting illegal continuations and adding back the legitimate short games for $n = 5, 6, 7, 8$ gives

$$\text{Total games} = 9! + \sum_{n=5}^{8}\bigl(M_n - (9 - n)!\,M_n\bigr) = 9! - 23 M_5 - 5 M_6 - M_7.$$

(The $M_8$ term drops out since $1! = 1$.)

Answer: $9! - 23 M_5 - 5 M_6 - M_7$
