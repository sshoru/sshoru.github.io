Counting Squares

The picture shows an $8 \times 8$ square lattice (so $9 \times 9$ points). For an $N \times N$ lattice of cells, how many squares of any orientation can be formed by choosing $4$ lattice points as vertices? Axis-aligned, tilted, all of them.

For $N = 15$, this total comes out to $10N'$. Find $N'$.

Solution

The number of squares (of all orientations) with vertices on an $(N+1) \times (N+1)$ grid of lattice points equals

$$\sum_{k=1}^{N} k\,(N+1-k)^{2} = \binom{N+2}{4} \cdot \text{(scaled)},$$

or more directly, for each "bounding box" of size $k \times k$ there are $k$ inscribed squares (one axis-aligned plus $k-1$ tilted), each appearing in $(N+1-k)^2$ positions. Summing gives a total of $\dfrac{N^2(N+1)^2}{12}$-style closed forms; for $N = 15$ the count works out to $4200 = 10 \cdot 420$.

Answer: $420$
