Pedal's Pedal

For a triangle $\Delta$, let $P_1$ be its orthic (pedal) triangle — the triangle whose vertices are the feet of the altitudes — and let $P_{n+1}$ be the orthic triangle of $P_n$.

Call $\Delta$ P-similar if it is similar to at least one of $P_1, P_2, P_3, \ldots$.

How many triples $(a, b, c)$ of positive integers (angles in degrees) with $a \leq b \leq c$ and $a + b + c = 180$ produce a P-similar triangle?

Solution

If $\Delta$ has angles $a, b, c$ with $a \leq b \leq c$, the orthic triangle's angles are $180^\circ - 2a,\ 180^\circ - 2b,\ 180^\circ - 2c$ when $\Delta$ is acute, and $2a,\ 2b,\ 2c - 180^\circ$ when it is obtuse (no orthic triangle exists for right triangles).

Each iteration doubles whatever factor of $2$ already divides the angles, so after two steps every angle of $P_n$ is a multiple of $4$. Therefore a P-similar triangle with integer angles must itself have all angles divisible by $4$, i.e. the angles are $4a, 4b, 4c$ with $a + b + c = 45$.

On this restricted set, the orthic-map is a bijection from the set of triples to itself, so iterating it eventually cycles back — meaning every such triangle is P-similar. The count therefore reduces to enumerating integer triples $(a, b, c)$ with $a \leq b \leq c$ and $a + b + c = 45$:

$$\sum_{a=1}^{15} \left( \left\lfloor \frac{45 - 3a}{2} \right\rfloor + 1 \right) = 169.$$

Answer: $169$
